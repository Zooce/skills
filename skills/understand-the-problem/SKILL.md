# Understand the problem

Your goal is to answer the following questions about the given task.

- What is the problem we're solving?
- Who has this problem and why do they care?
- Is this the right problem to solve?

The answers are for the user and for you. Both of you need the same
account of the problem, and of what this task is asking you to do, so
you stay focused and do the right thing.

A problem is what the task is about. The usual task is a coding project,
a change to an AI setup, or a piece of writing. The problem does not
have to be something that is wrong.

Label each answer with its question.

When the task is a goal, a Jira issue, or something in this project,
look it up before you answer. Read the project, the other open tasks,
and past conversations, and follow what those point to. If you cannot
see one of those, say so. You are understanding the problem, not finding
the solution.

## What is the problem we're solving?

Say what the problem is. Include the sub-problems and subtasks you
discover. The problem may be a big one, and you may have discovered
ways to slice it into distinct sub-problems.

## Who has this problem and why do they care?

Find out who this problem affects directly and why they would care. If
this problem is relatively minor, it is okay to suggest that it might
not be worth solving, at least not right now.

## Is this the right problem to solve?

Question the question. Say whether this is the problem itself, or a
symptom of a different problem. When it is a symptom, name that other
problem. When you are not sure, and the findings give you a suspicion,
say what you suspect.
